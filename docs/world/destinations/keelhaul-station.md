# Keelhaul Station

The Terran Union's principal outer-core shipyard is large enough to feel like
infrastructure rather than a station. Capital-class hulls move through construction
bays the size of small moons while repair crews cycle continuously through the
maintenance docks. The sound of it — distant impacts, pressure equalisation, the
low hum of industrial power systems — is constant and pervasive.

Civilian access requires a valid contractor permit. The application process takes
time and involves Union background checks that are thorough by any standard.
Pilots who clear the process gain access to a mission board weighted heavily toward
military logistics contracts: component delivery, personnel transport, supply runs
to outlying Union installations. The pay is consistently good and the paperwork
consistently dense.

The ship dealer at Keelhaul stocks military-specification vessels rarely available
through civilian markets — Union surplus, refurbished fleet units, and newly
commissioned designs not yet in general circulation. Prices reflect the quality
and the exclusivity. Captains who access the dealer without an existing relationship
with Union logistics command will find the sales staff attentive but not forthcoming.

Union vessels and credentialed contractors move through Keelhaul with rehearsed
efficiency. Everyone else learns quickly where they stand in the priority queue.
