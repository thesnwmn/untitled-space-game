# Veil Station

The Procyon Institute's main orbital installation is clean in the way that
institutional spaces are clean — not inviting, but precise. Veil Station orbits
Procyon II in a wide ring, its inner sections divided between research faculty,
data archives, and support staff quarters. The sections available to visiting
pilots occupy a smaller, carefully separated outer ring.

Archivist Dain manages the trade floor with the systematic thoroughness of someone
who catalogues as a reflex. Stock rotates slowly; the goods on offer reflect
Institute priorities rather than market demand. Pilots who need standard supplies
may find the selection narrower than expected. Pilots who need research equipment,
certified navigation data, or specialist technical components will find Veil Station
is one of very few places in core space that reliably stocks them.

The mission board runs heavily toward survey contracts, data retrieval runs, and
the administrative courier work that the Institute generates in quantity. Pay is
good, requirements are exacting, and mission briefings arrive in writing with
unusual thoroughness. The Institute expects its contractors to read them.

Entry to the inner campus requires Institute credentials. Requests for access
without them are declined in writing, with the relevant credential application
process included as an attachment.
